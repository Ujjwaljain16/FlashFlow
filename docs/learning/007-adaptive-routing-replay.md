# Stage 7 Learning Notes — Adaptive Routing & Counterfactual Replay

## Before Stage 7

Stage 6 turned "EWMA sometimes locks onto one target" into a mechanistic, quantified claim: tie-break order controls the outcome only when targets are exactly equal, collapsing toward chance-level selection the instant any real timing difference exists. It also built and validated the machinery — bootstrap CIs, effect sizes, rank-sum tests — needed to tell a real improvement from noise. What it didn't build was a routing policy that actually used more than one signal, or a way to ask "what would a different policy have done under these exact same conditions." Both were explicitly deferred, not overlooked: Stage 6's own exit artifact named them as the next stage's job, contingent on the statistical foundation existing first.

## The Design Gap

`trd.md` and `prd.md` specify six routing signals — Latency, Load, Health, Capacity, Cache, Cost — but neither document says how each becomes a concrete, correctly-directioned score component, what happens before a signal has any data, or how stale data should be treated differently from missing data. Every existing selector (RR, LC, EWMA, P2C) uses exactly one signal and has a cold-start rule discovered ad hoc through Stage 3's experiments (006-B: EWMA's "unobserved beats observed" rule causes hard lock-in). Building a six-signal router without resolving these questions first would have meant guessing at exactly the kind of unstated design decision Stage 3 spent four experiments uncovering. Separately, comparing routing policies fairly has always meant re-running an experiment once per policy with the *hope* that arrival timing, seeds, and failure schedules lined up identically — true by convention in 005-E/007-B's hand-written harnesses, never guaranteed by any type system.

## Signal Design

Health is deliberately **not** a scored signal. Every real and virtual harness since 005-D already filters candidates by `registry.IsAvailable` before a selector ever sees them — folding health into the score as well would duplicate a responsibility that already lives correctly outside every selector. Capacity is **not** a separate additive term: it's folded into Load as a utilization ratio (`load / capacity`), directly reusing Stage 3's own WRR lesson that raw connection counts without a capacity denominator are misleading across heterogeneous targets. Cold start is **neutral** (0.5), not optimistic — a direct, evidence-grounded contrast with EWMA's "unobserved beats observed" rule, which 006-B proved causes deterministic lock-in among identical targets. Staleness only discounts data with **positive evidence** of being old (a recorded `lastSelected` timestamp that has actually aged past `StaleAfter`) — an early draft treated "no staleness record yet" as equivalent to "stale," which would have wrongly penalized a target with perfectly good pre-existing data; caught and fixed before any test was written against it. Tie-breaking sorts candidates alphabetically before scoring, so identical scores never depend on map iteration order.

## Validation

007-A checked all of this against synthetic states before any real routing scenario touched it — the same discipline 006-A established for statistics. Six checks (load/latency/cost monotonicity, cold-start neutrality, staleness collapse, cache affinity specificity) all passed, with cold-start landing exactly at the 0.500 midpoint between an observed-bad (0.020) and observed-good (0.990) target, and staleness collapsing a stale-excellent and a stale-terrible reading to the identical 0.500 — proving old information is treated as *unknown*, not half-trusted.

## Real-Scenario Routing Findings

007-B ran the exact 005-E heterogeneous scenario (1 slow, 2 fast targets) plus a new homogeneous negative-case scenario. Adaptive avoided the slow target better than Least Connections and P2C (5.7% vs 14.0%/14.0% share) while splitting the two equally-fast targets nearly evenly (134/149) — exactly where EWMA still locked in hard (274/5), the precise failure mode this design was built to avoid. In the homogeneous negative case, Adaptive's distribution (101/100/99) was nearly indistinguishable from Round Robin's, while EWMA locked in anyway (290/5/5) even with zero heterogeneity to justify it — combining signals didn't manufacture an advantage or a pathology where none existed. 007-C then moved the ground under the router's feet (a 3-phase best-target swap, A→B→A), and Adaptive tracked the actual best target in all three phases, adapting within 2–9 requests each time. The mechanism was identifiable, not assumed: adaptation was too fast to be staleness-driven (the 1s threshold never had time to trigger within a 500ms phase) — the real driver was that the "losing" target kept receiving a residual 8–20% of traffic throughout, so its EWMA estimate stayed current on its own.

## Exploration/Recovery and Failure/Health

007-D deliberately isolated the mechanism 007-C's gentler scenario never exercised: genuine staleness-driven rediscovery of a target that stops receiving traffic entirely, not just a "losing" one. The first attempt (a 2000ms "terrible" service time for the losing target) failed outright — not from a staleness bug, but because that target's single in-flight request pinned its Load counter at 1 for virtually the entire run, maxing out its utilization penalty independent of any staleness effect. Diagnosed by reading the raw trace rather than assumed, and fixed by using a moderate, self-clearing 200ms bad phase instead. The redesigned scenario showed real rediscovery (a 205ms gap between selections exceeding the 150ms `StaleAfter` threshold), and post-improvement recovery that was real but gradual (10% share in the first 200ms) — because a staleness reset clears a target's *score* but not its EWMA-smoothed *estimate*, which only tracks new truth one observation at a time. 007-E then confirmed `AdaptiveSelector` needs zero health-specific code at all: B was selected zero times during a confirmed-unhealthy window (by construction — it's never a candidate), and on return, the same staleness mechanism 007-D validated turned out to handle "welcome back" for free, resetting B's stale latency estimate to neutral rather than carrying any bias, with traffic parity restored immediately (20/20/20).

## Counterfactual Replay Engine

`internal/replay` formalizes what 005-E/007-B's harnesses already did by convention: a `Scenario` (exogenous — target service times, arrival schedule, failure schedule, RNG seed) is a plain immutable value, and `RunWorld` constructs a completely fresh selector, trackers, and `*vtime.Engine` per call via `PolicySpec.New` — no two calls ever share a mutable object. Three properties were verified directly, not assumed from the design: identity (same Scenario+PolicySpec+seed produces a byte-for-byte identical trace — checked using P2C specifically, since its randomness is the one part that could leak from a shared source), divergence-only-after-intervention (two scenarios sharing identical health-probe machinery but differing in one failure's ground truth produce identical traces up to the failure's actual effect and never before it), and isolation (an unrelated scenario run in between two identical runs cannot change the second run's outcome). The divergence test's own first draft was a false positive worth keeping in mind: comparing a scenario with no health registry at all against one with a failure diverged trivially at t=0 from the mere presence or absence of `health_probe` trace events, not from the failure itself — fixed by giving both scenarios identical always-on health machinery via a `UseHealthRegistry` flag. 007-F re-verified all three properties as a recorded, auditable experiment on 007-B's real scenario, matching the 006-A/007-A precedent that unit tests establish internal correctness while a recorded experiment establishes the same claims as evidence.

## Policy Comparison

007-G ran one shared Scenario — heterogeneity plus a temporary failure — through all five policies via `RunWorld`, the engine's actual payoff. Health eligibility held for every policy with zero exceptions after detection, generalizing 007-E's finding beyond Adaptive: it lives entirely in upstream filtering, outside any selector. One genuine, minor edge case surfaced during verification and was traced rather than dismissed: a single EWMA selection landed at the exact same virtual instant its own probe first recorded the target UNHEALTHY — an artifact of the engine's same-timestamp event ordering (arrivals are scheduled ahead of the health Ticker's own recursively-scheduled next firing), not a filtering defect. The experiment's own prediction — that only Adaptive would show a measurable recovery-transition gap — did **not** hold: the observed gap (1.7 points) was the same order of magnitude as every other policy's structurally-impossible "gap" at that window size. The reason is itself the finding: 007-D's undershoot requires the recovering target's *true performance* to change alongside its data going stale; here only health changed, so a stale-then-neutral score reconverges to the same already-correct value within a handful of requests, too fast for a 300ms window to see. The staleness mechanism is still exclusively Adaptive's; its *visible cost* depends on how much the recovering target's actual performance diverged from what its stale data implied, not merely on whether a reset occurred.

## Statistical Robustness

007-H closed the loop back to Stage 6: 007-B's slow-target-avoidance comparison (Adaptive 5.7% vs Least Connections 14.0%) was a single run. 30 replicates, each varying only in arrival-timing jitter (±2ms, seeded per replicate), with both policies run against the byte-for-byte identical jittered Scenario within each replicate — a paired design, exploited correctly by bootstrapping the 30 per-replicate differences directly (`BootstrapCI` on the differences, not `BootstrapDiffCI` treating the two policies as unpaired samples, which would have discarded exactly the pairing structure that makes a paired design powerful). Result: 30/30 replicates favored Adaptive, mean diff 8.12 points, 95% CI [8.01, 8.23] entirely excluding zero — landing almost exactly on 007-B's single-run estimate (8.3 points), confirming that observation was a reliable read of the underlying effect, not a lucky draw.

## Surprises

1. **007-D's first failed attempt was more informative than a clean success would have been.** An extreme "badness" parameter (2000ms) created a load-counter artifact that had nothing to do with staleness — caught by reading the raw trace, not by assuming the mechanism must be broken or must be working.
2. **007-G's central prediction was wrong, and that was the more valuable result.** The staleness-driven undershoot is real (007-D) but not automatic — it requires the recovering target's true performance to have actually changed, a distinction no single-scenario experiment could have revealed without a head-to-head comparison across all five policies on identical conditions.
3. **`internal/replay`'s own divergence test caught itself.** Its first draft diverged at t=0 for a reason that had nothing to do with the causal claim being tested (instrumentation-shape mismatch, not the intervention) — a reminder that a test asserting "these differ" needs exactly as much scrutiny as one asserting "these match."
4. **007-H's multi-seed mean landed almost exactly on 007-B's single-run number** (8.12 vs 8.3 points) — arrival-timing jitter alone doesn't meaningfully perturb which target these largely deterministic policies favor, so the single run had already been a reliable read; the paired study converted that from a reasonable assumption into a checked claim.

## Limitations

- Every Adaptive finding in this stage uses the default `AdaptiveConfig` (`StaleAfter`=1s, `ReferenceLatency`=100ms, default weights). No sensitivity analysis across alternative weightings was performed — out of scope per the stage's own instruction not to build tuning machinery prematurely.
- The Cache signal is router-maintained key-affinity (last server to serve a given key), not introspection into any real cache's actual contents — an intentional, stated simplification, not a claim about real cache behavior.
- `internal/replay`'s divergence guarantee has been demonstrated for a health-failure intervention specifically; it has not been separately checked for every other kind of exogenous change a future Scenario field might introduce.
- 007-G's recovery-transition comparison used one specific window size (300ms / 60 requests); the experiment's own finding is that this granularity was too coarse to see Adaptive's undershoot even where 007-D showed it exists under a different scenario shape — a stated boundary of what this comparison can detect, not a claim the effect doesn't exist at any granularity.
- No DR-OPE, dashboard, or tuning machinery was built, per this stage's explicit scope boundary — `internal/replay` supports counterfactual comparison of a fixed set of policies on a fixed Scenario, not off-policy evaluation of a policy different from the one that generated the data.

## Evidence Discipline

**Strong** (deterministic, replicated, mechanism identified): all six 007-A signal-validation checks; the health-eligibility finding, confirmed across all five policies with zero exceptions after detection (007-E, 007-G); `internal/replay`'s identity/divergence/isolation properties, verified both as unit tests and as a recorded experiment on a real scenario (007-F); the 007-H paired multi-seed result (95% CI entirely excluding zero, 30/30 sign-consistent). **Suggestive, precisely characterized**: 007-D's staleness-driven rediscovery and gradual recovery (demonstrated on one designed scenario, not yet replicated across seeds the way 007-H replicated the slow-target-avoidance finding). **Unresolved, explicitly flagged**: whether the staleness-driven recovery undershoot would become visible at a finer measurement granularity than 007-G's 300ms window, on the same failure-only (no true-performance-change) scenario shape.

## Stage 8 Motivation

Stage 7 built a router that combines six signals transparently and explainably — every scoring decision traceable to a specific, evidence-grounded design choice, none of them a black box — and a replay engine that can now answer "what would policy B have done under the exact conditions policy A saw" with a proven guarantee, not a hopeful convention. It also demonstrated, concretely, both what that machinery is good for (007-G's honest correction, 007-H's statistically-supported robustness claim) and where its current boundary sits (recovery-transition visibility depends on measurement granularity and on whether a target's true performance actually changed, not just its health). That is precisely what a tuning, evaluation, or dashboard layer would need underneath it: a router whose behavior is already understood well enough to know what "improved" would even mean, and a replay engine already proven not to leak state between the counterfactuals it's asked to compare.
